The mild pejorative *yankee* first appears in a 1765 American poem, in which it means "person from New England". During the American Revolution, *yankee* typically referred to Americans in general. The term's New England origin influenced its meaning of "northerner" during the American Civil War. Later that century, *yankee* meant specifically Protestants, as opposed to immigrant Catholics.

The clipped form *yank*, in evidence since 1778, still means "person from the US" in global English today. The Bronx baseball team New York Highlanders was renamed New York Yankees in 1913. Their iconic Yankee Stadium was built in 1923. In 1956, *Yankee* was selected to represent the letter Y when the NATO phonetic alphabet was first codified. *Yankee* standing for Y was common usage in telephone spelling since 1945. Prior to that, the word representing Y was typically Yokohama.

The origin of the word *yankee* is disputed. Historically, it was attributed to a Native American language. A British officer writing in 1789 traces it to Cherokee "eankke", meaning "slave" or "coward". An 1822 overview of Native American history and cultures claims the original term was "Yengees". Today, it is generally agreed that those explanations were false. The likely origin of *yankee* is the Dutch name Janke, a diminutive of the popular name Jan. The usage of "Yankey" to refer to a nonspecific Dutchman is found as early as the 1680s.

The Dutch given name Jan owes its popularity to its prominence in the Bible. In Dutch translations, John the Baptist, John the Apostle, and his Gospels of John all bear the name Jan. In the original Greek, his name is rendered Ἰωάννης (Iōánnēs). Iōánnēs was a popular Jewish given name 2100-2400 years ago. It appears in the Old Testament as יוֹחָנָן (Yôḥānān), meaning "God has been gracious".

Unsurprisingly, John and its local equivalents has been a popular name across Christendom and Islam for thousands of years. Some names that are also John are Ian (Scottish), Ivan (Russian), Jovan (Croatian), Yahya (Arabic), Giannis (Greek), Giovanni (Italian), Jean (French), Johan (Dutch), Hans (German), Sean or Shane (Irish), João (Portugese), Juan (Spanish), and Evan (Welsh). Some common women's names that originated from John and friends are Joan, Joanna, Jane, Janet, Janice, Jean, and Jeanne. Jack used to be an English nickname for John, but has since split off into its own separate name.

Surprisingly, the given name Jon is unrelated to John. It is short for Jonathan, which is admittedly also a Biblical name of Hebrew origin that begins with יהוה (YHWH).
